1. Organized Refund Fraud (Refund-as-a-Service)
The Attacks: Criminal syndicates (such as REKK, RBK, Artemis, and Simple Refunds) operate on platforms like Telegram to steal high-value goods (GPUs, laptops, etc.). They utilize social engineering, file fake police reports, initiate "empty box" return scams, launch phishing campaigns against corporate employees, and actively bribe logistics and customer service insiders.  

The Countermeasures: Amazon utilizes advanced machine learning anomaly detection, files aggressive civil litigation through its Counterfeit Crimes Unit (CCU) to unmask operators, and collaborates with the FBI's "Operation Chargeback" to drive federal indictments.  

2. "Black Hat" Seller Sabotage
The Attacks: Rogue sellers sabotage competitors using Listing Hijacking (undercutting prices with fakes), Listing Content Manipulation (injecting prohibited medical or offensive terms to trigger automated compliance suspensions), False IP complaints, Variation Abuse, and bot-driven PPC Click Fraud.  

The Countermeasures: Implementation of real-time machine learning validation, Brand Registry IP tools, and CCU lawsuits targeting entities that abuse the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO).  

3. AI Review Brokers & Social Proof Fabrication
The Attacks: Malicious actors utilize "Brushing" scams (mailing empty envelopes to real addresses to generate fake verified shipping data) and deploy Generative AI tools to mass-produce hyper-realistic 5-star reviews for themselves or 1-star review-bombing campaigns against competitors.  

The Countermeasures: Amazon blocked over 275 million suspected fake reviews proactively, partnered with the Better Business Bureau (BBB) for joint lawsuits against broker domains, and updated internal policies to instantly purge automated bot accounts. The FTC also introduced strict federal rules banning this conduct.  

Key Dates:

August 2024: The FTC promulgated a Final Rule explicitly banning fake, AI-generated, and incentivized reviews.  

2024: Over 275 million suspected fake reviews were blocked by Amazon's systems.  

August 2025: Amazon secured a massive legal victory, seizing more than 75 fraudulent website domains operated by a global fake-review network.  

March 2026: Implementation of Amazon's strict "Agent Policy" targeting AI-automated review bots.  

4. Supply Chain Counterfeiting & Commingled Inventory
The Attacks: Counterfeiters exploited Amazon's "FBA Commingling" system, where identical items from different sellers were mixed in shared warehouse bins based solely on UPC barcodes, making the source of fake goods untraceable.  

The Countermeasures: Amazon officially ended commingled inventory for brand owners, mandating unique FNSKU labels cryptographically tied to specific sellers to establish a physical chain of custody. They also launched the "Transparency" serialization program and joined the Anti-Counterfeiting Exchange (ACX) to share data on bad actors across the retail industry.

5. First-Party Platform Practices & Deceptive Interfaces
The Attacks/Issues: The platform itself faced regulatory action for utilizing "dark patterns" to enroll customers into Prime subscriptions without clear consent, alongside creating a labyrinthine cancellation process. Antitrust regulators also targeted Amazon's algorithms for coercing sellers to artificially inflate prices on competing external websites.  

The Countermeasures: Severe regulatory intervention, including a $2.5 billion settlement with the FTC and an antitrust lawsuit filed by the California Attorney General.  


6. E-Infringement & Intermediary Liability (Beverly Hills Polo Club Case)
The Attacks: Amazon's private-label apparel brand ("Symbol") utilized a logo strikingly similar to the Beverly Hills Polo Club (BHPC) mark, resulting in massive brand dilution.  

The Countermeasures: MLLM (Multimodal LLLMs)

Key Dates:

2018–2020: Origins of the dispute and discovery of the infringing Symbol branding.  

February 2025: The Delhi High Court awarded a record ₹340 crore ($39 million) in damages to Lifestyle Equities.  

July 2025: The execution of the multimillion-dollar decree was temporarily stayed by a Division Bench.  

October 2025: The Supreme Court of India dismissed appeals to lift the stay, freezing the damages pending a full hearing.
